# Brain Bureau Operations — Series Bible
### The shared identity, the variety levers, and the paper-based element for every future Operation

This document exists so that Operation 3, 4, 5… don't have to reinvent the format from scratch, and so they don't accidentally end up feeling like the same game reskinned. It has three parts: the fixed chassis every Operation shares, the variety matrix of things that should deliberately change each time, and the paper-based working-out element that now sits alongside every Operation.

---

## 1. The fixed chassis (stays the same every time)

- **Brain Bureau framing**: students are Agents, briefed by a Handler, working a case file.
- **Length and format**: a standalone challenge completable in one ~45-minute sitting, individually or in a small group. **Marked exception — the Extended Operation** (first: Tailwind): a single-player Operation designed for ~60–75 minutes, delivered as two sittings with board state persisting between them. One lesson stays the default definition; an Extended Operation is a deliberate, named variant, not drift.
- **Visual base**: dark, terminal/dossier-style UI (see the visual identity discussion) with one signature accent colour per Operation layered on top.
- **Shape of the ending**: a final unlock moment, a light/silly reveal of who or what was behind the mystery, and a certificate.
- **The paper companion** (new — see Section 3): every Operation ships with a printed/printable working-out booklet, not just a digital interface.

Everything below this line is a lever, not a rule — the point of the bible is to force a deliberate choice on each one every time a new Operation is planned, rather than defaulting to whatever the last one did.

## 2. The variety matrix

### 2.1 Narrative engine (the "genre" of the mission)

| Engine | Shape | Pacing | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **System malfunction** | A machine/system is broken; fix it before shutdown | Timed, urgent | **Used — Zero Hour** |
| **Whodunnit / detective** | A cast of suspects or a data set; eliminate them one by one | Untimed, curiosity-paced | **Used — Prime Directive.** Modelled on "The Mystery of the Burgled Bangers" |
| **Undercover infiltration** | The agent is inside the target organisation posing as staff; the pressure is maintaining cover, not surviving | Soft-pressure, no clock | **Used — Encore** |
| **Heist-in-progress** | A villain's plan is mid-execution; agents intercept before a final step completes | Timed, framed as "catching up" | Unused — e.g. decoding a shipment manifest before it leaves the docks |
| **Rescue / recovery** | A missing agent or object; follow a trail of location-based clues | Untimed, exploratory | **Used — Tailwind** (a tagged Arctic tern gone silent mid-migration; reconstruct her route and re-acquire the signal) |

### 2.2 What the puzzle answers assemble into (the "final lock")

- **Numeric code** (keypad) — **used, Zero Hour**.
- **ID / text entry** — a single correct identifier typed into a terminal. **Used, Prime Directive**.
- **Sequence/order** — answers arranged in the right order to reveal a route, a message or a running order. **Used, Tailwind** (drag the migration's waypoints into flight order at the dish console, paired with a destination choice so the combined answer space is ~480 and unguessable). Strongest when the ordering is itself a curriculum task rather than a shuffle — Tailwind's route order is the geography.
- **Word/phrase** via a letter-code (a=1, b=2…), modelled on Burgled Bangers' "CULPRIT" / "PUG" reveals. Unused.
- **Image reveal** — each correct answer removes a layer of redaction from a picture, or a reflection/rotation puzzle assembles a hidden message (Burgled Bangers Clue 4). Unused.
- **Map coordinate / grid reference** — answers plot a point that reveals a location. **Used, Encore** (camera grid + timestamp: pick the feed, dial the time).

### 2.3 Villain / antagonist archetype

- Rogue saboteur with a grudge — **used, Zero Hour** (ZERO).
- A well-meaning but bumbling apprentice who caused chaos by accident — **used, Prime Directive** (COGSWORTH).
- A rival organisation that planted someone inside — **used, Encore** (Vellum Records, via the mole Remy Kade). *Vellum is deliberately reusable as a recurring series antagonist.*
- No villain at all — a natural/technical problem (a data storm, a broken satellite, a jammed signal). **Used — Tailwind** (a solar storm; the comic reveal is Agent Colin, a pigeon roosting on HQ's own aerial).
- A friendly NPC who needs help rather than someone to catch. Unused.

### 2.4 Signature colour & motif

Each Operation keeps one accent colour and one or two unique icons, so a gallery of Operations reads as a matched set that's still individually recognisable at a glance.

- **Zero Hour** — amber on charcoal; countdown/vault motif.
- **Prime Directive** — amber on charcoal; case-file and evidence-tag motif.
- **Encore** — electric magenta on surveillance greyscale; REC-dot and camera corner-bracket motif (the stage neon is the only colour on the camera wall).
- **Tailwind** — signal-red on slate/petrol blue with a cream map; map-pin-and-red-string motif, radar-sweep secondary.

**Watch the drift:** the first two Operations both landed on amber-on-dark and both used a dark industrial world. Encore deliberately breaks that; Tailwind stays clear of both amber and magenta. When planning the next Operation, check this list before choosing a palette — "dark and amber" is the series' default gravity and needs resisting, not repeating.

### 2.5 The interface frame

Distinct from palette: the *metaphor* the whole screen adopts.

- **Zero Hour** — terminal/vault UI.
- **Prime Directive** — a physical case file, pages added as the dossier thickens.
- **Encore** — a surveillance camera wall: a grid of corrupted feeds cleaned one lock at a time, ending in playback mode. (The first wearer of the `surveillance-feed` archetype.)
- **Tailwind** — a Situation Room wall: one explorable scene (wall map, pinned documents, red string) whose evidence fills in as clues resolve, ending at the dish console. (The first wearer of the `situation-room` archetype.)

The strongest frames are the ones where the final lock *is* the interface rather than a separate minigame — Encore's pick-the-camera-dial-the-time playback set the bar; Tailwind's console (the route string draws itself between the pins as you sequence them) follows it.

### 2.6 Structure (new lever — how the clues are arranged)

- **Linear chain** — clues in a fixed order, each gating the next; progress UI is digits/fragments slotting into the final lock. **Used — Zero Hour, Prime Directive, Encore.** Best when the narrative benefits from fixed pacing, and for the one-lesson format.
- **Open board** — all clues visible and workable in any order from the start (point-and-click adventure structure); the only gate is the final lock, which needs N findings; the board filling up *is* the progress UI. **Used — Tailwind.** Best for exploration-paced engines (rescue/recovery), text-rich Operations, and single-player Extended delivery; supports redundancy (corroborating clue pairs where either member unlocks the shared finding) so one stuck clue never hard-blocks a solo player.

Choose deliberately, like every other lever. An open board needs: per-clue self-containment (no answer feeding another clue), a findings tracker with persistence, and flavour hotspots so exploration is rewarded between solves.

## 3. The paper-based element (new)

### 3.1 Why

Left purely digital, students can be tempted to guess-and-check their way through a keypad rather than actually calculating — especially with a countdown running. A paper element makes the working-out visible, gives the teacher something to check afterwards, and matches the two example resources this series is based on, both of which are paper-first with clue cards.

### 3.2 What it is: the Agent Field Notebook

A short, printed (or print-at-home) booklet that travels alongside the digital Operation:

- One page per challenge with a dedicated **"Show your working" box** — the digital site should deliberately give minimal or no on-screen scratch space, so the incentive to use the notebook is real rather than decorative.
- A **running code strip** at the bottom of each page where the student writes the digit/letter they've derived, building towards the final code entry — so by the end they have their own paper record of the full code before they ever type it in, which also gives them something to double-check against if the digital keypad rejects an attempt.
- **Open-board variant — the Evidence Log** (first: Tailwind): for a non-linear Operation the running code strip becomes an any-order Evidence Log — one working box per board item with a "finding filed" tick, a candidates table with a "ruled out because…" column (the teacher's best window into reasoning), and a route/answer strip completed before the final console entry.
- A **reusable prop, not a worksheet**: rather than one bespoke notebook per Operation, design a single standard-issue "Bureau Field Notebook" template once, with just the per-challenge working boxes changing each time. Agents get to know the object itself, the way a real recurring prop would.

### 3.3 Paper-only mechanics worth building in (things a screen can't easily do)

A few mechanics are genuinely better on paper, and using them is a good way to make the notebook feel essential rather than a compliance step:

- **A build-your-own decoder wheel** (two concentric card discs, printed once, cut out and kept by the student) — a classic physical-spy prop that can be reused across every future Operation for letter-shift ciphers, rather than being single-use.
- **A colour-filter/overlay reveal** — a coloured acetate strip or highlighter-style reveal page where a hidden message only becomes visible when the right coloured layer is placed over patterned "noise," similar to the reflection/rotation reveal in the Burgled Bangers pack. This can't be replicated as satisfyingly on screen.
- **A coordinate-grid or table lookup page**, mirroring the multiplication-grid substitution cipher used in the Burgled Bangers "Confession" page — genuinely easier to work across by hand/ruler than by scrolling on a small screen.

### 3.4 How it interacts with the digital site

- The website should only ever ask for the **derived answer** (a digit, a letter, a word) — never the raw calculation — so the actual maths has to happen on paper.
- Consider a light "evidence check": before the final keypad accepts entry, prompt "Have you filled in your Field Notebook code strip?" as a soft nudge rather than a hard gate.
- Answer keys for teachers should map directly onto the notebook's per-challenge boxes, so marking is just checking working against a page, exactly like the two example resources' answer-key pages.

## 4. A short research note: activity types worth coding into future Operations

To keep the four-challenges-in-a-row shape from feeling monotonous, it's worth drawing on a wider set of interaction types than "read a question, type a digit." Digital escape-room and Breakout EDU-style resources commonly draw from a fairly small, reusable toolbox of mechanics — worth treating as a component library to pick from per challenge, rather than building one generic question format:

| Mechanic | How it works | Good fit for |
|---|---|---|
| **Combination lock styles** (number, word, colour, or direction lock) | Borrowed directly from physical Breakout EDU kits — a lock that opens on a number, a word, a sequence of colours, or a sequence of directions/arrows | Any challenge whose answer is short — good as the *final* lock of an Operation, varied instead of always a plain numeric keypad |
| **Drag-and-drop sequencing/ordering** | Students drag items (numbers, steps, cards) into the correct order | Ordering/comparing numbers, sequencing a method, ranking |
| **Matching pairs** | Students connect two related items (a question to its answer, a term to its definition) | Vocabulary, factor/multiple pairs, equivalent representations |
| **Hover-to-investigate + click-to-zoom** ⭐ **BUILT** | Hoverable hotspots on a scene highlight on mouseover and zoom into a detail view on click. Built during the Prime Directive build and available to reuse. | Examining evidence closely, "search the room" moments, rewarding agents who explore a scene rather than only reading the question. Works in any Operation with a rendered environment. Tailwind uses it as the chassis interaction for the whole board. |
| **Click-to-reveal hotspots** | Clicking hidden points on an image reveals clues, like a digital scavenger hunt | Narrative/flavour beats, easing in a briefing — and pure flavour hotspots (Tailwind's mug, radio, DO-NOT-OPEN drawer) that reward exploration without advancing the case |
| **Image transform puzzles** | Rotating or reflecting a shape/grid reveals a hidden pattern or message on screen | Geometry-adjacent objectives, or purely for visual variety between numeric challenges |
| **Sorting/classification** | Dragging items into labelled bins or a Venn diagram | Factors/multiples/primes, classifying numbers, true/false sorting — and word-class or figurative-device classification (Encore, Lock 2) |
| **Cipher decoding** | A=1 letter codes, Caesar/shift ciphers, simple substitution grids, or word-substitution codes (e.g. Encore's antonym cipher), where a derivation unlocks a letter or word | Spelling out a word or phrase as the "final lock" (see 2.2), or vocabulary objectives in costume |
| **Coordinate/grid puzzles** | Plotting or reading grid references to find a location or a hidden value | Data, geometry, or as a stylised version of the multiplication-grid cipher mechanic. Tailwind: lat/long pin-drop plotting and six-figure grid references on inset maps |
| **Data lookup/cross-reference** | A data table (like the dog data table in Burgled Bangers) that must be cross-referenced against clues to narrow down an answer | Whodunnit-engine Operations (2.1); Tailwind's ring registry and krill survey table |
| **Timed/locked-reveal sequencing** | A challenge only unlocks once a previous one is solved, or after a countdown beat, to control pacing | Building tension before the final reveal |
| **Short audio/video briefing clips** | A Handler "video call" or audio clue rather than text | Briefing/debrief moments, to break up reading-heavy screens |

The recommendation isn't to use all of these in one Operation — it's to make sure any two consecutive Operations don't lean on the same two or three mechanics, the same way the narrative engine and final-lock type should be varied deliberately (Sections 2.1–2.2).

## 5. Using this bible

When planning a new Operation:

1. Pick the curriculum unit and its target objectives (White Rose for maths; National Curriculum programmes for other subjects — Tailwind pairs KS2 geography with Y6 science).
2. Choose a narrative engine (2.1) different from the last Operation used.
3. Choose a final-lock type (2.2) different from the last Operation used.
4. Choose a villain archetype (2.3), a signature colour/motif, and at least two digital mechanics (Section 4) not used in the immediately preceding Operation.
5. Choose a structure (2.6): linear chain or open board — and if open board, decide the findings gate and any corroborating pair.
6. Build the paper companion page-by-page alongside the digital content, using the shared notebook template (or its Evidence Log variant for open-board Operations).
7. Write the answer key mapped to the notebook.
